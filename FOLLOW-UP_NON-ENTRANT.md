# Stage 2 Live Interview Guide: Non-Entrants Blocked by Burden

**Cohort definition.** A1 = `No`, plus H2 = `Too complicated`, `Too much effort`, or `Don't trust myself to manage it`, plus a substantive H3.

**Explicitly excluded.** H2 = `Don't trust the platforms` or `Returns don't justify it`. Those are real answers but they describe a market you would have to create rather than one you would serve. They get screener follow-ups, not calls.

Target 25 to 30 minutes. Shorter than the active-manager script because there is less history to reconstruct.

---

## Why this cohort is different, and why it is dangerous

Every other guide reconstructs something the person actually did. This one cannot. These people have no LP history, which means almost everything they tell you about LPing is imagination.

That makes them the easiest cohort to mislead yourself with. A non-entrant will happily agree that a simpler tool would bring them in, because agreeing costs them nothing and the statement will never be tested. Stated intent from people who have never done the thing is the weakest evidence in the entire study.

So the interview is not asking whether they would use a simpler product. It is asking two things they can actually answer from experience:

1. **How far did they actually get?** Reading, watching, opening the app, connecting a wallet, hovering over deposit. Distance traveled is behavior, not opinion, and the exact step where they stopped is the finding.
2. **What are they doing with that money instead?** Wherever the capital sits right now reveals their true effort tolerance, risk tolerance, and yield hurdle, all without them having to estimate any of it.

If someone never got past watching a video, and their capital is sitting in a CEX savings product at 4%, then complexity is not what stopped them. Complexity is the socially acceptable answer. The real answer is that the whole category is below their attention threshold, and a simpler product does not fix that.

**Rules:**

- Never ask "would you use it if." Ask what they did, where they stopped, what they chose instead.
- Do not explain how LPing works. The moment you teach, you have converted an interview into a demo and every answer afterward is worthless.
- If they ask you a question about how something works, defer it to the end. Write down that they asked, and what they asked. Their questions are data about the knowledge gap.
- When they say "too complicated," never accept it. Ask what specifically, and when they encountered it.

## Section 1: Who they are on-chain

- What do you actually do on-chain today
- What is the most complex thing you have done: swaps, bridging, staking, lending, minting, governance
- What chains and what wallets
- How long have you been around
- Roughly what portion of your net worth is on-chain

Establishing competence first matters. Someone who bridges across four chains and runs a validator is not blocked by complexity in any general sense, and if they still say LPing is too complicated, that is a specific and very useful signal about this specific activity. Someone whose entire history is buying on an exchange is telling you something different.

## Section 2: Where the money is instead

- Where is your idle capital sitting right now
- Is it earning anything? What, and roughly what rate
- How did you pick that
- How much attention does it need from you
- What is the most effort you currently put into any yield position
- Have you ever moved capital because something better came along? What did that take

**This is the anchor section of the whole interview.** Their current yield is their revealed hurdle rate. The attention their current position requires is their revealed effort budget. Together those two numbers tell you whether an LP product is even in their consideration set, and they are far more reliable than anything they say about what they would be willing to do.

If they hold a low-yield passive position and describe LP returns as attractive, but have never moved, the gap is not complexity. It is that the expected value did not clear their activation threshold. Find out where that threshold is.

## Section 3: The approach and the stop

This is the core. Go slow here.

- When did you first hear about providing liquidity
- What made you look into it
- What did you actually do to learn about it: videos, threads, docs, friends, tried a testnet
- Did you ever open a DEX interface? Which one
- **How far did you get?** Reading, opened the app, picked a pool, connected a wallet, got to the deposit screen
- What was on the screen when you stopped
- What were you unsure about at that moment
- Did you go looking for an answer? Where, and did you find one
- What would have had to happen right then for you to continue
- Did you come back later, or was that it
- Have you looked again since

The furthest-progress question and the what-was-on-screen question are the two that pay. A person who bailed at the fee tier selector has a completely different problem than a person who bailed because they could not decide which pool, who has a different problem again from someone who never opened an app at all.

## Section 4: Unpacking the block

Branch on what they said in H2. Never accept the category label without a specific instance underneath it.

### If "too complicated"

- What specifically was confusing
- Was it the concepts, the interface, the decisions, or the risk
- Which decision did you not know how to make
- **Do you know what happens to your position if the price moves a lot?**
- What did you not understand that you wish someone had explained
- Have you learned anything about it since? What changed

That fourth question is not a test, it is a diagnostic. If they can describe impermanent loss accurately and still did not enter, the block is not knowledge. If they cannot, the block is education, and education is a content and onboarding problem rather than an automation problem. Those are different companies.

### If "too much effort"

- What did you think would be required of you
- Where did that impression come from
- How much time would have been acceptable
- What do you currently spend that much time on
- Is it the ongoing time, the setup time, or the unpredictability of when it will demand attention
- **Would it matter if you never had to react to anything, only set it up once?**

Distinguishing setup cost from ongoing cost from interrupt cost matters a great deal for what the product has to be. Interrupt cost, the fact that a position can demand attention at an unpredictable moment, is the one automation genuinely removes. The other two it only reduces.

### If "don't trust myself"

- Trust yourself to do what, specifically: pick the pool, set the range, react in time, not panic
- Have you lost money on-chain before? What happened
- What do you do now when you are unsure about a decision
- Do you follow anyone or copy anyone
- Would you be more comfortable if something else made the decisions, or if it just stopped you making a bad one
- Would you want to be able to override it

The distinction between wanting a decision-maker and wanting a guardrail is the entire product question for this branch, and the screener cannot see it.

## Section 5: The counterfactual, carefully

Only after Sections 1 through 4 are done. These questions are leading by construction, which is why they come last and get discounted in analysis.

- If someone handled all of it and you just deposited, would you have done it
- What would you need to see before you put real money in
- How much would you start with
- What would that have to return before it was worth doing at all
- Would you want to understand what it was doing, or not care as long as it worked

Record the answers. Weight them lightly. Compare them against Section 2, because if someone says they would start with $10K but their entire on-chain footprint is $2K, you have measured enthusiasm rather than intent.

## Section 6: Close

- What is the most annoying thing about using DeFi generally, for you
- Is there something you have wanted to do on-chain and could not figure out how
- Anything I should have asked
- Who else should I be talking to

The second question is the discovery escape hatch for this cohort. Non-entrants often have a clearer view of where DeFi is unusable than actives do, because actives have already absorbed the friction and stopped noticing it.

## Reading the aggregate

**Progress distribution.** Plot how far each respondent got, from "never opened an app" through "reached deposit screen." If the mass sits at the shallow end, the block is upstream of your product entirely and you are looking at an awareness and education problem. If the mass clusters at a specific late step, that step is a concrete, fixable design target and probably the most actionable finding this cohort can produce.

**Hurdle rate versus effort budget.** For each respondent, pair their current yield with the attention their current position demands. A cohort sitting at 4% and near-zero attention has an activation threshold your product must clear on both axes simultaneously. That is a hard product to build and worth knowing early.

**Knowledge diagnostic split.** Among the "too complicated" group, count how many could describe what happens when price moves against a position. High accuracy means the block is not education, and something else is going on. Low accuracy means your first product may be a teaching interface that happens to automate, and that is a very different roadmap.

**Setup versus ongoing versus interrupt.** Among the "too much effort" group, count which of the three they name. Only interrupt cost is squarely addressed by automation. If most name setup cost, the answer is onboarding, not automation.

**Guardrail versus decision-maker.** Among the "don't trust myself" group, count the split. It maps directly onto Assumption 3 and it comes from a cohort with no incentive to flatter your thesis.

**The credibility check.** Compare Section 5 stated entry amounts against Section 1 on-chain footprint. Systematic inflation across the cohort means the whole section is enthusiasm and should be discarded, not discounted.

## Per-interview capture

- Cohort tag: `non_entrant_burden`, plus H2 sub-branch
- On-chain competence: most complex action performed, chains, tenure, approximate on-chain footprint
- Current idle capital location, yield, and attention required
- Revealed hurdle rate and revealed effort budget
- First exposure to LPing: when and how
- Furthest progress reached, on the scale from never-opened to deposit-screen
- What was on screen at the stop point
- Whether they searched for an answer, and whether they found one
- Whether they returned or looked again since
- Knowledge diagnostic: could they describe price-movement consequences, yes or no
- For effort branch: setup versus ongoing versus interrupt, which named
- For trust branch: guardrail versus decision-maker, plus override requirement
- Stated entry amount from Section 5, and the ratio to actual on-chain footprint
- Questions they asked you, verbatim, and at what point
- Unusable-DeFi item named at close, if any
